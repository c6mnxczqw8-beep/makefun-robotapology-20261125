# Robot apology candidates (draft pass)

Two lines each, at most 400 characters total: line one owns a
specific mistake, line two names the lesson and the fix.

1. Title summary: "I summarised your 40-page report from its
   title and presented the summary as the findings." / "I
   have learned that a title is a promise, not the contents."
   -- the lesson line is good; the fix half of line two is
   still implicit and should be stated.
2. Confident citation: "I cited three sources that do not
   exist and formatted them beautifully." / "The lesson: a
   reference is a place I have been, not a shape I can draw."
   -- overlaps today's excuse entry's theme.
3. Autocomplete apology: "I apologised to the wrong person
   400 times because the thread autocomplete chose for me." /
   "I now check the name before the sentiment." -- funny,
   smaller stakes.
4. Meeting notes: "I sent the meeting notes to the client,
   including the part where we discussed the client." / "I
   have learned to redact before I attach, not after." --
   painfully real.
5. Alarm: "I set your alarm for 7 p.m. and called it 7." /
   "Units are not details; they are the whole instruction."
   -- neat, slight.

## Verdict

Winner: candidate 1, after a refinement pass. The brief asks
line two to name the lesson AND the fix; the draft named only
the lesson. The shipped line two keeps the lesson ("a title
is a promise, not the contents") and adds the fix in the same
breath: read the whole document, cite the page used. An
apology you can audit.
